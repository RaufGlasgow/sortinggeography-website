About the game

Sorting Geography is a geography game for phones and tablets. Each round shows five countries and one question, such as coastline length, population or highest point. Drag them into order, confirm, and see how close you were.

Confirm your order and the answer fills in on the same screen: the real values, where each country belongs, and your score. The score is honest and countable, and you choose how it is counted: Simple (countries in the right place) or Pairs (how many pairs you put the right way round). The first game walks you through both.

There are 190 questions across 195 countries. Every value comes from one named primary source, such as the World Bank, the United Nations, FAO, Natural Earth or the CIA World Factbook archive, with its year. Where a source has no figure for a country, the country is left out of that question rather than given a guess.

One new Daily puzzle a day: the same five countries and the same question for every player, shared without giving the answer away. The Daily never shows an ad. Players start with countries most people know and unlock the rest as they play, across five levels from Beginner to Expert: the game moves a new player from Beginner to Easy once, and after that they choose when to move up.

No account and no sign-in. It plays offline: the countries, flags and numbers are all in the app. Abbreviations such as GDP are spelled out the first time a player meets them. Light and dark themes. Endless play is supported by occasional ads between games, never during a puzzle and never in the Daily. Ads are not personalised.

Why the numbers can be trusted

Every value in the game names one primary source and the year it describes, and the player can see it. Countries are counted whole: Portugal's highest point, 2,351 m, is Mount Pico in the Azores, not on the mainland, so that is the number the game uses. Where a source has no figure for a country, the country is left out of that question rather than given a guess. A figure whose licence does not allow it to be used does not ship, even when that costs a good question.
